3. Did any research memo change a decision?

This is where the week's real finding is, and it reverses what I have been reporting. Research is *not* advisory: kismat/engine.py:38 sets RESEARCH_WEIGHT = 0.4, memos carry 40% of the combined score, and 263 journal decisions show combined != systematic. I asserted the opposite on 6 September and repeated it for two weeks without re-reading the code.

But no trade has turned on it: no entry was pushed below the 0.35 threshold, all 12 sells were trend breaks or trailing stops, and there are zero rotation fills. Measurable P&L impact: zero.

The mechanism that matters: a flat memo scores exactly 0.0 — conviction is discarded — so it applies a 40% haircut rather than expressing "no view". Writing *no memo* is the neutral act.

A correction to my own claim from yesterday. I wrote that "every memo is a drag, including the longs", and lumped the two cases together. That was over-stated. A long at conviction 0.45 against a systematic 0.65 is the blend doing exactly what a blend should — the desk saying "this deserves less than the trend score implies" — and shrinking one estimate toward another is a feature, not a bug. The defect is narrower than I said: it is flat → 0.0 specifically. The fix in research/proposals/2026-09-19-conviction-means-two-things.md §1 stands; recommendation §2 (cut RESEARCH_WEIGHT) is weaker than I made it sound and should not be actioned on yesterday's reasoning alone.
