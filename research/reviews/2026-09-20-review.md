## 3. Did any research memo change a decision?
**This is where the week's real finding is, and it reverses what I have been reporting.** Research
is *not* advisory: `kismat/engine.py:38` sets `RESEARCH_WEIGHT = 0.4`, memos carry 40% of the
combined score, and 263 journal decisions show `combined != systematic`. I asserted the opposite on
6 September and repeated it for two weeks without re-reading the code.

But no trade has turned on it: no entry was pushed below the 0.35 threshold, all 12 sells were
trend breaks or trailing stops, and there are zero rotation fills. Measurable P&L impact: **zero**.

The mechanism that matters: **a `flat` memo scores exactly 0.0** — conviction is discarded — so it
applies a 40% haircut rather than expressing "no view". Writing *no memo* is the neutral act.

**A correction to my own claim from yesterday.** I wrote that "every memo is a drag, including the
longs", and lumped the two cases together. That was over-stated. A long at conviction 0.45 against a
systematic 0.65 is the blend doing exactly what a blend should — the desk saying "this deserves less
than the trend score implies" — and shrinking one estimate toward another is a feature, not a bug.
**The defect is narrower than I said: it is `flat → 0.0` specifically.** The fix in
`research/proposals/2026-09-19-conviction-means-two-things.md` §1 stands; recommendation §2 (cut
`RESEARCH_WEIGHT`) is weaker than I made it sound and should not be actioned on yesterday's
reasoning alone.

## 5. One thing to change, one thing to keep
**Change — turn rotation on at margin 0.30, but only after fixing `flat → 0.0`, and in that order.**
This week's backtest is unusually clear. On the whole 50-symbol universe over 530 days:

| configuration | return | Sharpe | max DD | trades | win rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| rotation **off** (live) | +14.1% | 0.65 | −12.9% | 203 | 38% |
| rotation **margin 0.30** | **+18.1%** | **0.76** | −13.0% | 246 | 39% |

Four points of return and a better Sharpe for no extra drawdown. **But rotation compares the best
candidate against the weakest holding by *combined* score — and today the weakest holdings are
weak only because a flat memo zeroed 40% of their score.** XLE (0.330), DBC (0.324), XLV (0.288)
and AAPL (0.288) all sit below the 0.35 entry threshold for that reason alone, while AVAXUSDT
(0.60) and AMD (0.59) carry full systematic scores because nobody has written about them. Turn
rotation on today and the engine would sell the researched names to buy the un-researched ones.

That ordering — fix the scoring, then enable rotation — is the single most valuable thing in this
review, and neither half is visible from either document on its own.

**Keep — naming invalidation levels in advance and honouring them.** Two fired cleanly this week:
CSL.AX flipped to long on 17 September when both legs I specified on the 15th were met (a close
above A$175 *and* a broker raising — RBC upgraded and lifted its target A$148 → A$213), and ETH's
2,700 held through a drawdown. That machinery works even while the scoring around it does not.
