**Headline: nine closed trades, nine losses, −$97.25, average hold 2.4 days.** Equity went

## 1. What worked, and was it skill or luck?
Nothing realised worked. Every closed trade lost. The only positions in profit are open ones:
DBC +2.7%, EEM +1.2%, AMD +1.2%, XLK +1.0%.

The one honest claim the desk can make is negative: it called **flat** on every name that was
subsequently stopped out — ANZ.AX, AAPL, AMZN, QQQ, XLV, SPY, BHP.AX. That looks like skill and
mostly is not. In a week where the systematic layer opened twelve positions and exited nine at a
loss, "no edge here" was right by construction. The real test is whether flats beat longs, and
that test is still unflattering: both long calls on the book are underwater, and the desk's long
memos are **2 good out of 4 judged**.

Also worth saying plainly: DBC, the best-performing position, is one the desk called flat twice.

## 2. What lost money, and which rule or memo was wrong?
By size: **LINKUSDT −$37.45**, AAPL −$15.45, QQQ −$15.80, XLV −$6.33, BHP.AX −$5.35,
XLF −$5.21, ANZ.AX −$4.90, AMZN −$4.09, SPY −$2.66.

No memo caused any of them — every entry was made by the systematic layer. But two memos were
wrong on their own terms:
- **`research/memos/2026-09-07/LINKUSDT.json` (morning), long 0.55.** Revised to flat that
  evening on whale distribution; LINK then fell from 13.455 to the stop at 11.72. The morning
  call would have been wrong and the evening revision was right for the reason it gave.
- **`research/memos/2026-09-07/AAPL.json`, flat 0.60.** Right about the exit, wrong about the
  thesis: I asserted a sell-the-news base rate without checking it. Apple dipped 1% on event day
  and is now ~332, about 5% above the exit. The base rate I failed to look up (Bank of America:
  gains in the 60 days after an iPhone reveal in 17 instances since 2007) points the other way.

**The rule that is wrong is the exit, not the entry.** Buy decisions score **4 good of 5 (0.80)**;
sell decisions score **0 of 4 (0.00)**. Seven of nine exits were trend breaks totalling −$54.44,
two were trailing stops totalling −$42.80. The entries are defensible; what follows them is not.

## 3. Did any research memo change a decision? Was the change good?
Still no — nothing in the engine acts on a `flat`. But the data now leans one way for the first
time. From `outcomes.json`, `by_memo_at_entry`:

| memo at entry | trades | total P&L | per trade |
|---|---|---|---|
| flat | 5 | −$23.33 | **−$4.67** |
| no memo | 4 | −$73.91 | **−$18.48** |

Trades entered with a flat memo lost about a quarter as much per trade as trades entered with no
memo at all. With n = 9 this is suggestive, not proof — LINKUSDT alone is half the no-memo
column — but it is the first evidence that the research layer carries information, and it is
exactly what `research/proposals/2026-09-06-memo-gated-entries.md` predicted.

## 4. Are stops too tight, too loose, or right?
**The holding period and the signal are mismatched.** This is a 55-day-breakout, 63-day-momentum
system with an average hold of **2.4 days**. Exits by reason:

| reason | n | P&L |
|---|---|---|
| trend break (below fast SMA and underwater) | 7 | −$54.44 |
| trailing stop hit | 2 | −$42.80 |

Two names — XLV and XLF — were bought and exited on the **same day**, XLF within two hours. That
is not a stop being too tight; that is an entry and an exit rule disagreeing with each other.
Note the trend-break rule cannot simply be removed: `research/backtests/2026-09-06.md` has the
whole universe at +16.7% with trend-break 1d against +2.3% with it off. The problem is the
interaction, and it needs a test rather than an opinion.

## 5. One thing to change, one thing to keep
**Change — populate the `rs` column.** This is the highest-value fix available and it is
evidence-backed. The packet's signal table has carried an `rs` header with `-` for all 49 symbols
for three consecutive days, and `prompts/pattern_analyst.md` names rs as an input it should use —
so the pattern analyst has been working blind and I have been deriving relative strength by hand.
Meanwhile this week's study, `research/backtests/study-2026-09-12.md`, ranks **`+rs 0.15` first of
ten variants by robustness (0.79 against live 0.53)** with better out-of-sample return (+27.1% vs
+25.8%). The desk is failing to compute the one signal its own research ranks highest. Written up
as `research/proposals/2026-09-13-populate-rs.md`.

**Keep — writing "flat" without embarrassment.** Twenty of the twenty-three memos this week were
flat, and the flat-memo column above lost a quarter as much per trade as the no-memo column.

## 6. Calibration
| conviction band | memos | judged | good | hit rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| high (≥0.7) | 0 | 0 | 0 | — |
| mid (0.5–0.7) | 6 | 4 | 2 | 0.50 |
| low (<0.5) | 1 | 0 | 0 | — |

**The desk has not written a single memo above 0.7 conviction in eight days.** Given 0-for-9 that
restraint is probably correct, but it means the high band is entirely untested — the calibration
question the desk rules care about ("0.9 means you would be shocked to be wrong") has no data
behind it yet. Mid-conviction calls are running at a coin flip, which is what conviction near 0.5
is supposed to mean, so the numbers are at least honest.
